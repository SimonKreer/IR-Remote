# Technical Foundations - Universal Infrared Remote Control

## 1. The Brain: Heltec ESP32 (U1)

The **Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 (V2/V3)** serves as the central processing unit. It is based on the powerful ESP32 SoC (System-on-a-Chip).

- **Processing Power:** With a dual-core processor and high clock frequencies, it enables precise capture and generation of microsecond-level pulses required for IR protocols.

- **Peripherals:** The integrated **0.96" OLED display** is controlled via the **I2C bus**. This enables user-friendly menu navigation for device profile selection and operation modes.

- **Connectivity:** WiFi and Bluetooth capabilities offer potential for future Smart Home extensions.

---

## 2. Physical Basis: Infrared Communication

Data transmission occurs wirelessly using light waves in the infrared range (~940 nm), just above the human-visible spectrum.

- **Modulation:** To prevent interference from sunlight or room lighting, signals are modulated with a **carrier frequency** (typically **38 kHz**). A "logical 1" is represented by modulated pulses, while "logical 0" is represented by gaps (absence of carrier).

- **Protocols:** The firmware distinguishes between **protocol-based data** (e.g., NEC or Sony standards) and **RAW data**, where only the time intervals between pulses are recorded.

---

## 3. Transmission Stage (Infrared Emitter)

The transmission stage converts electrical signals from the microcontroller into strong light pulses.

### IR Transmitter LED (IR1 - TSAL6200)

This high-performance LED is optimized for maximum emission in the 940 nm range. Since transmission range is directly dependent on the current, the diode operates near its specification limits.

### Switching Transistor (Q1 - 2N2222A)

Since ESP32 GPIO pins cannot supply sufficient current (45–100 mA required) for extended range, the **2N2222A NPN transistor** acts as an electronic switch.

- **Operation:** Minimal current at the base pin (controlled by ESP32) switches the collector-emitter path through, closing the main current circuit of the LED.

- **Base Resistor (R1):** Protects the microcontroller from overload and ensures the transistor operates in saturation mode (fully conducting).

---

## 4. Reception Stage (Infrared Sensor)

The **TSOP38238 (LED1)** is a specialized receiver for modulated infrared signals.

- **Internal Signal Processing:** The component contains a photodiode, amplifier, and bandpass filter. The filter eliminates all signals not matching the carrier frequency, providing excellent immunity to ambient light and electrical noise.

- **Demodulation:** The sensor output provides a clean digital signal that can be read directly by the ESP32.

- **Supply Filter (R6 & C2):** To minimize interference from other electronics, the receiver is stabilized via an **RC filter** (low-pass filter). This prevents power supply noise from affecting reception quality.

---

## 5. Power Supply Stabilization and Decoupling

In a circuit where components like IR LEDs draw high pulse currents, power supply stabilization is critical.

- **Capacitors (C1 - C4):**

  - **Ceramic Capacitors (100 nF):** These suppress high-frequency noise generated by rapid digital switching.

  - **Electrolytic or Larger Ceramic Capacitors (10 µF):** These serve as local energy reservoirs (buffers). They prevent system voltage from dropping during transmission operations, which would weaken the IR signal.

---

## 6. User Interface (HMI)

Interaction occurs through four mechanical **SMD buttons (U2–U5)**.

- **Internal Pull-Ups/Pull-Downs:** The ESP32 has integrated resistors (typically **30 kΩ to 80 kΩ**) on nearly all GPIO pins.

- **Menu Logic:** The firmware translates button presses into navigation commands on the OLED display, allowing users to select between "Learn" (sniffing) and "Send" (transmission) functions.
